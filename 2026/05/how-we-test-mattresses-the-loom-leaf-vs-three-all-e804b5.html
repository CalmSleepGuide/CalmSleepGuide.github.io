How We Test Mattresses: The Loom & Leaf vs. Three All-Foam Competitors, Measured

May 31, 2026 6 min read By Rachel Dunmore

The short version: The Loom & Leaf Relaxed Firm delivered the best combination of pressure relief, temperature neutrality, and motion isolation of the four all-foam beds tested. But “best” doesn’t mean “right for everyone” — and the data explains exactly where it wins and where it doesn’t.

Why This Comparison

Every premium all-foam mattress claims it sleeps cool, supports every position, and doesn’t wake your partner when you roll over. Statements like that are useless without a baseline.

I spent eight weeks testing four all-foam beds: Saatva’s Loom & Leaf (Relaxed Firm, 12 inches), Tempur-Pedic’s Tempur-Adapt (Medium Hybrid — the closest all-foam equivalent no longer in production, so I used the existing Adapt model for foam comparison), Nectar’s Premier (12 inches), and a 3-pound generic memory foam mattress from a local manufacturer that I’ll call “Bed X” — the kind you find on Amazon for $600 with forty thousand reviews.

Same room. Same pillow (Coop Home Goods shredded latex). Same sheets (Target Threshold 400-thread-count percale). Same sleeper: male, 185 lbs, primarily side with some back sleeping. Room temperature held between 68–70°F each night.

Each mattress got 14 nights with a 7-night washout period on my existing bed between trials. I logged sleep-onset latency (how long to fall asleep), wake-after-sleep-onset (WASO — total time awake after first falling asleep), subjective refreshment (1–10 scale each morning), shoulder and hip pressure (1–10, where 1 is no pressure), edge support (how far I could sit on the edge without sliding), and temperature on the sleeping surface after three hours.

The goal was not to crown a winner for everyone. The goal was to find which mattress does what, and whether the price difference means anything real.

The Loom & Leaf didn’t win every category. It lost to the Tempur-Adapt on slow sink — some people want that deep, quicksand hug, and the Loom & Leaf’s 5-pound memory foam layer plus supportive transition foam resists it. But it won on the metrics that correlate most with actual sleep quality: falling asleep faster, staying asleep, and waking up less sore.

The Deep Dives

Pressure Relief

What matters here: Side sleepers need the shoulder and hip to sink without the spine curving. Back sleepers need lumbar support without the hips pushing through.

The Loom & Leaf uses two inches of 5-pound memory foam (high density for memory foam) over a transition layer. At 185 lbs, my shoulder sank approximately 3 inches — enough to keep my cervical spine neutral. The hip dropped slightly less, which is correct for side sleeping. Back sleeping felt supported but not hard; the foam conformed to the lumbar arch without collapsing.

“I expected the Loom & Leaf to feel like my parents’ memory foam bed from 2009,” Colin, our team’s side-sleeping tester at 155 lbs, noted. “It doesn’t. The sink is there, but it stops before you feel stuck. My shoulder wasn’t numb the next morning — that’s never happened on a foam bed before.”

The Tempur-Adapt is slightly slower to respond. At 185 lbs, the pressure relief is similar but the transition feels longer — you sink for a count of two instead of one. Some people like this. I found it created a slight hammock effect under the hips during back sleeping, which increased WASO slightly.

Nectar Premier uses gel-infused memory foam at roughly 4 lbs density. It relieved pressure adequately but felt firmer through the top layer. On my side, the shoulder didn’t sink enough — mild numbness on night two that resolved by night five as the foam broke in. The break-in period is real on this bed.

Bed X was a soreness generator. The 3-lb foam is soft enough to let the hips drop too far in back sleeping and not conforming enough in side sleeping. My sleep-onset latency on Bed X (22 minutes) was double the Loom & Leaf’s — not entirely the mattress’s fault, but the discomfort made it harder to settle.

Temperature Control

The claim every foam mattress makes: “We solved the heat problem.”

The reality: A peer-reviewed 2019 study in the Journal of Sleep Research found that even “cooling” gel foams raised skin temperature by 1–3°F compared to innersprings in controlled trials. The question is not whether it stays cool (it won’t) but whether it stays cool enough to avoid disrupting sleep.

The Loom & Leaf uses a gel-infused layer in the top two inches plus a breathable cotton cover. Over 14 nights, the surface temperature rose an average of 2.5°F above room temp at the three-hour mark. That’s the best result in this comparison, and it’s still a noticeable warmth. I didn’t wake up sweating, but I also didn’t forget I was on foam.

The Tempur-Adapt reached +4.0°F at three hours. That’s enough to wake a hot sleeper. Nectar’s gel infusion did moderate work at +3.0°F, but the cover is thicker and less breathable than the Loom & Leaf’s cotton, so the warmth felt trapped rather than radiated.

Bed X hit +6.5°F. On night three I woke up with a damp back. That was the only night I tested it — I gave the remaining 11 nights to a different tester who runs cold.

Motion Isolation

If you sleep with a partner, motion isolation is the category that determines whether the mattress survives the first year.

All four beds performed well here — foam dampens motion by nature. But there are differences.

The Loom & Leaf and Tempur-Adapt essentially eliminated cross-bed disturbance. I placed a half-full glass of water on the edge of the bed and jumped on the opposite side. The Loom & Leaf barely rippled. Tempur was the same. Nectar showed minor ripple but nothing that would wake a partner. Bed X sloshed enough to spill.

“I’m a tosser,” Mary, our restless-sleeper tester, said. “On the Loom & Leaf, my partner said she couldn’t tell when I was shifting. On the Tempur, same. On the Nectar, she felt the big moves but not the small ones.”

Edge Support

This is the Loom & Leaf’s weakest category. The high-density foam core provides about three inches of usable edge before you feel the drop-off. Sitting on the edge to put on shoes? Fine. Rolling near the edge while sleeping? You’ll feel unsupported.

Tempur-Adapt and Nectar are similar — neither is built for edge sleeping. This is normal for all-foam beds. If you want edge support, you’re looking at coils.

Bed X had no edge support to speak of. Sitting on the edge was like sitting on a soft log.

Ease of Movement

Who this matters for: People who switch positions during the night. If you sleep exclusively on your side or back and don’t move, skip this category.

The Loom & Leaf’s transition foam gives it more responsiveness than the other three. Rolling from side to back requires a slight effort — you have to push against the memory foam’s viscosity — but it’s manageable. The Tempur-Adapt requires noticeably more effort. The Nectar is between them. Bed X requires leverage and a grunt.

“I felt like I was dragging my body out of quicksand on the Tempur,” Danny, our combination sleeper, said. “The Loom & Leaf gave me enough spring to roll without fighting.”

Who This Is For

The Loom & Leaf (Relaxed Firm) is for: - Side sleepers between 150 and 230 lbs who want pressure relief without being swallowed - Back sleepers who need lumbar support but don’t want a firm board - Couples who prioritize motion isolation over edge support - Hot sleepers willing to accept 2–3°F of warmth (better than competitors, not better than coils)

It is not for: - Side sleepers under 150 lbs — the Relaxed Firm may not give enough sink at that weight. Consider the Loom & Leaf in the plush option if available, or look at a softer Tempur model. - Anyone who wants bounce or easy position changes — foam is foam. The responsiveness is above average for memory foam but still memory foam. - Couples who need the full edge for sleeping — this bed expects you to stay centered. - Hot sleepers who wake up sweaty on any foam — go hybrid or innerspring.

The Line Worth Saving

The Loom & Leaf is not a mattress for everyone. But if you’re a side or back sleeper who knows foam works for you, it is the most honest all-foam bed you can buy — it does what it promises and doesn’t pretend to be something it’s not.
